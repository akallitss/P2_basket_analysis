1. Overview

This report documents the VMM3a ASIC configuration scan performed during the CERN SPS beam test in November 2025 using the P2 basket detector system. The primary goal was to determine the optimal combination of analogue gain (sg) and peaking time (snt) that maximises the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) across all detector VMMs.

A secondary analysis characterises the trigger performance by identifying beam spill structure from trigger VMMs 0 and 1, and comparing hit rates during spill-on and spill-off periods across all gain/peaking-time configurations.

Key result: The configuration sg = 3.0 mV/fC, snt = 100 ns yields the highest VMM-level SNR for 6 out of 8 detector VMMs, with SNR values ranging from ~19 to ~40. The remaining two VMMs (14 and 15 on P2 Large) marginally prefer sg = 4.5 / snt = 100 ns. The channel-level analysis favours sg = 3.0 / snt = 100 ns for 7 of 8 VMMs.

2. Detector Setup & VMM Mapping

The P2 basket detector consists of three gaseous detectors instrumented with VMM3a ASICs on an ALINX readout board. Each detector is connected to one or two VMMs. Two additional VMMs (0 and 1) read out plastic scintillator trigger counters.

3. VMM Configuration Scan Analysis

3.1 Methodology

The analysis pipeline (orchestrated by vmm_config_scan_analysis.py) consists of four stages:

  1. Noise baseline estimation — from sng=1 (neighbour-trigger suppressed) runs. Robust median absolute deviation (MAD): σnoise = 1.4826 × MAD, computed only on hits with over_threshold=0 and adc > 20.
  2. Signal MPV estimation — from sng=0 (self-triggered) runs. The most-probable value (MPV) of the ADC distribution is extracted as the smoothed histogram peak, restricted to hits with over_threshold=1 and adc < 1023 (non-saturated).
  3. SNR computation — at VMM level and per-channel level: SNR = MPV / σnoise. The VMM-level noise cut (= mean + 10σnoise) is used as adc_min for the MPV search at both levels.
  4. Best-config summary — the configuration maximising SNR is identified independently at VMM level and channel level; agreement between the two is flagged.

3.2 Run Configuration & Scan Parameters

Six gain × peaking-time combinations were scanned. For each configuration a sng=0 (signal) run and a sng=1 (noise) run were recorded. The beam rate during signal runs was approximately 5 kHz (muons). Up to 5 ROOT files were merged per run to improve statistics.

3.3 Noise Baseline Estimation

Noise is characterised from sng=1 runs where only above-threshold hits are recorded but the neighbour-suppression flag separates genuine signal from baseline fluctuations. The robust estimator (MAD-based σ) is preferred over the standard deviation because it is insensitive to ADC artefacts such as the occasional ADC=16 spike.

A noise quality flag is assigned to each (VMM, config) pair: ok — σnoise < 9 ADC counts; warn — 9 ≤ σ < 12 ADC counts (elevated noise, usually at short peaking times); bad — σ ≥ 12 ADC counts (excluded from best-config selection).

3.4 Signal MPV Estimation

The signal amplitude is characterised by the most-probable value (MPV) of the ADC spectrum, extracted as the peak of a Gaussian-smoothed histogram. Only hits with over_threshold=1 and ADC above the VMM-level noise cut are included. Non-saturated hits (adc < 1023) are selected to avoid pile-up at the ADC ceiling.

3.5 SNR Results

The SNR is computed at both VMM level (using VMM-averaged noise and MPV) and per-channel level (individual channel noise and MPV). The VMM-level SNR provides a single figure-of-merit per configuration, while the channel-level view reveals intra-VMM uniformity and problematic channels.

The channel-level median SNR is the more robust estimator and consistently points to sg = 3.0 / snt = 100 ns.

4. Trigger Analysis

4.1 Trigger Setup

The trigger system consists of two plastic scintillators read out by VMMs 0 and 1 (directly connected, no external delay module). The reference trigger channel is VMM 0 channel 48. A second scintillator is connected to VMM 0 channel 40 and VMM 1 channels 0, 20, 40, and 60.

The trigger analysis (vmm_trigger_analysis.py) uses the trigger hit time stream to identify SPS beam spill structure and compute hit rates in beam-on (spill-on) and beam-off (spill-off) periods separately for each detector VMM and each configuration.

Timestamps are in 1 GHz clock ticks (1 ns per tick). Corrupted timestamps (ticks ≥ 2 × 1012) are removed before analysis. VMM timestamps are sorted chronologically before rate computation.

4.2 Spill Structure Identification

Hit rates are binned in 1 ms windows. During SPS extraction, the trigger rate rises sharply to ~4 kHz (beam-on) for a spill flat-top of ~2.5–4 s, then drops to a noise floor ≪ 1 kHz during the inter-spill gap. A threshold of 1 kHz cleanly separates spill-on from spill-off bins. Contiguous above-threshold regions shorter than 1 s are reclassified as spill-off (start-of-run artefacts).

4.3 Hit Rates vs Configuration

For each (sg, snt) configuration the mean detector VMM hit rate is computed separately during spill-on and spill-off periods. The spill-on rate contains both signal and electronic noise; the spill-off rate gives the pure noise floor. A high ratio rateon / rateoff indicates good signal-to-background.

The spill rates table (Appendix C) summarises the spill-on and spill-off rates in kHz for all VMMs and configurations, together with the trigger reference rate and the number of 1 ms bins classified as spill-on and spill-off.

5. Conclusions & Recommendations

Recommended configuration: sg = 3.0 mV/fC, snt = 100 ns
This setting maximises the VMM-level SNR for 6 of 8 VMMs and the channel-level median SNR for 7 of 8 VMMs. It also gives a well-controlled noise baseline (σ < 9 ADC for all VMMs) while preserving signal MPV in the range 130–190 ADC.
  • Peaking time: snt = 100 ns is the sweet spot — snt = 50 ns increases noise without a proportional gain in MPV; snt = 200 ns reduces noise only marginally compared to 100 ns while the SNR does not improve.
  • Gain: sg = 3.0 mV/fC avoids noise inflation that appears at higher gains (sg = 4.5 or 6.0) particularly at short peaking times. For the P2 Large VMMs 14/15, sg = 4.5 + snt = 100 ns is competitive at the VMM level but the channel-level data consistently favour sg = 3.0.
  • Trigger performance: The spill-on / spill-off rate ratio is highest at sg = 3.0 / snt = 100 ns for all three detector groups, in agreement with the SNR analysis.
  • Saturation: No saturation artefacts (ADC = 1023 pile-up) were observed at sg = 3.0 / snt = 100 ns; at sg = 6.0 a small fraction of channels approach the ceiling, motivating the lower gain choice.
Note on ADC = 16 artefact: A spike at ADC = 16 is present in noise runs at short peaking times (snt = 50 ns). This is an electronic artefact of the VMM3a ASIC and is excluded from the noise baseline computation by the robust MAD estimator (not affected by the spike if it is a minority of samples). At snt = 100 and 200 ns the artefact is absent or negligible.
