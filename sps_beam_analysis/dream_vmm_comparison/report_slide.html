Why the VMM loses the weak pads — and DREAM does not

P2_OUT, SPS H4. VMM3a run_46 / cfg_gain4.5_peaktime200 against DREAM eff_nominal_1, matched operating point (mesh 450 V, drift 750 V), same uRWELL reference tracks, same selection, 53 pads under the beam.

The pad-to-pad gain variation belongs to the chamber. Both readouts measure the same map — per-pad, r = +0.94, a factor 3.9 from the weakest illuminated pad to the strongest, laid out as one smooth gradient across the beam spot.

What the VMM adds is a threshold that sits inside the Landau. Fitting one level to the DREAM spectra reproduces the VMM's efficiency pad by pad — 53 pads, one free number, r = +0.90, residual 5.1 points, predicted overall 0.855 against 0.853 measured. That level is 0.69× the most probable pulse of an average pad, which is 1.19× the most probable pulse of a weak one — so on the weak pads it eats the peak, not the tail.

The VMM's apparently better uniformity is an artefact of the same two effects. Detector spread 0.42 (DREAM, everything kept) → 0.36 once the threshold cuts the Landau → 0.26 once the VMM ADC's 43-count additive offset is folded in. Removing that offset from the VMM medians gives 0.36 — the truncated DREAM number. Nothing is left over.

One level is the first-order story, not the last word. Inverting the same model pad by pad instead of jointly gives 53 independent thresholds that centre on the global one (median 177 against 162 ADC) but scatter around it by 26 % rms — far beyond the 4 ADC that counting statistics allow. At 10 ADC per efficiency point that scatter is the 5.1-point residual quoted above, in other units. Read it as one common level plus a per-channel dispersion of order 26 %, which is what an untrimmed VMM front end is expected to have.

That it is quantitatively the whole story

Testing it pad by pad, with the averaging off

The ridgeline above is eight curves for 53 pads: each row is a gain band, the track-weighted mean of six or seven pads' unit-area spectra. That is the right average for the model — a band's sub-threshold area is exactly the mean of its pads' — but it cannot answer "do the cut-offs line up?", because it has averaged over the axis the question is about. A joint least squares has the same problem: it will always return some minimising T. So the model is inverted separately for every pad, solving effVMM = effDREAM × F(T) with that pad's own spectrum and its own two measured efficiencies, and the 53 answers are compared with the single global line.

The mechanism survives this: the 53 independent answers centre on the global value, with median 177 ADC (IQR 149–209) against the fitted 162. What does not survive is the stronger reading, that one number is the whole story. The honest statement is one common level plus a channel-to-channel dispersion of about 26 %. That dispersion shows no significant trend with pad gain (r = +0.24, p = 0.09), which is the reason it does not touch the conclusions below: every fix is a move in the ratio T/gain, and a gain-independent dispersion around T survives all of them equally. A dispersion that did track gain would have been a second mechanism and would have changed them.

Options, cheapest first

  • Raise the VMM front-end gain from 4.5 mV/fC. The discriminator is set in mV after the amplifier, so 9 mV/fC halves the threshold in charge — exactly the factor of two the model asks for — and it is a register change with no HV risk. Two things to check on the bench: the noise scales with the gain too, so sdt may not be able to stay where it is; and the 10-bit ADC saturates earlier, which costs the top of the Landau (it does not cost efficiency).
  • Lower sdt. All six P2_OUT chips already sit at 224, the lowest value used anywhere in the July setup (P2_IN ran 224–300). How much further it can go is a noise question this measurement does not answer — the cheap experiment is a threshold scan, 224/208/192, one chip, with beam.
  • Raise the mesh voltage. 93% at ×2 and 91% at ×1.5. It moves the whole Landau up, so it works, but the gain gradient is multiplicative and survives — the pads stay as unequal as they were, they just all clear the bar.
  • Use the per-channel trim DACs against the gain map, not for it. The usual point of trimming is to remove threshold dispersion; here the dispersion that matters is the detector's gain, and it is measured. Trimming the low-gain pads down would flatten the efficiency map directly. The range is a few mV, which is small against the factor 3.9, and it buys noise on exactly the channels that get the lower threshold — worth costing, not obviously worth doing.
  • Fix the chamber. The gradient is ~13 % per 10 mm across the beam spot and it is the same in both readouts, so it is amplification-side, not electronics. That is the only option that improves the detector rather than the readout's tolerance of it.

What this does not rule out

  • The two runs are five days apart. The gain map could have moved between them; that it did not is an inference from the r = +0.94 agreement, not an independent measurement.
  • The fitted level is a charge threshold expressed in DREAM ADC. It absorbs anything else that scales with pulse height and bites at the low end — time-over-threshold requirements, the peak-finder's own floor — so "the discriminator" is the natural reading, not a proven one.
  • The 26 % per-pad threshold dispersion is measured but not attributed. Untrimmed VMM channel-to-channel threshold spread is the obvious candidate, and the per-channel trim DACs would settle it on the bench in an afternoon; timewalk, per-channel noise, and any pad-dependence of the DREAM peak-finder would all land in the same number. Nothing here separates them.
  • The additive 43-count offset is measured, not explained. A pedestal in the peak ADC is the obvious candidate; a charge-injection scan would settle it in an afternoon.
  • Only P2_OUT. P2_MID and P2_IN have their own threshold settings and, in P2_MID's case, 27 dead channels on VMM 12; nothing here transfers to them without redoing it.
  • Everything is conditional on a uRWELL track pointing at the pad, so it is a statement about efficiency for tracks, not about noise occupancy at a lower threshold — which is the cost side of every option above and is not measured here.
