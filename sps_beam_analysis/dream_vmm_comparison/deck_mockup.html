Three slides: why the VMM is less efficient than DREAM

A mock-up for MPGD2026. Three 16:9 slides, almost no body text — each one is a picture with a headline and a single grey line of provenance. They are built by figures_deck.py from the same tables as the full note, so the numbers move together.

Sized for a projector, not a desk: type is ~2× a printed figure's, the pad maps drop their tick labels for a scale bar, and slide 3 runs 6 gain bands instead of eight so the rows are tall enough to read from the back. Colour does two separate jobs and they are kept apart — which readout is categorical (DREAM orange, VMM blue, on the dots, bars and lines), while what is mapped is a sequential ramp that changes with the quantity: purple for efficiency on slide 1, amber for gain on slide 2. Both maps on a slide share one ramp and one colorbar, which is what makes them comparable. Each ramp passes the ordinal checks against the slide background, including the one that matters in a hall: the pale end clears 2:1 contrast, so it does not wash out to paper.

Slide 1  — the observation

Say: same chamber, same beam, same mesh and drift voltage, the same uRWELL tracks and the same selection — only the electronics differ. DREAM records 95.6 % of the tracks that point at a pad; the VMM records 85.3 %. Then point at the right-hand panel: the deficit is not a uniform scale factor. DREAM holds every live pad above 92 %, while the VMM runs from 96 % down to 50 % — and the pads it loses are all in one corner.

Slide 2  — whose fault is it?

Say: the same corner, now in pulse height. The gas gain rolls off by a factor 3.9 across the beam spot, one smooth gradient, and both readouts measure the same map — pad for pad, r = +0.94, same direction to 5°. So the variation is the chamber's, not the electronics'. Leave them with the one loose end that sets up slide 3: the VMM's copy of that map is systematically flatter.

Slide 3  — the mechanism, and the close

Say: every row is the same Landau; the axis is logarithmic, so a gain factor is a pure sideways shift. The blue line is the VMM's discriminator — one level, all six chips at sdt = 224. Walk down the rows: the solid orange area is what falls below it. On the strong pads that is the bottom of the tail; on the weak pads the peak itself has slid onto the line. The bars on the right are the consequence — DREAM holds 98 → 92 % across the 6 gain bands, the VMM falls 93 → 63 %.

One fitted level, 162 DREAM ADC, cut into DREAM's own per-pad spectra reproduces the VMM's efficiency pad by pad: r = +0.90, 85.5 % predicted against 85.3 % measured. And it closes slide 2's loose end: cutting the spectra at that level is also what flattens the VMM's copy of the gain map.

What to say next — the options, cheapest first

  • Raise the VMM front-end gain. A register change. It halves the threshold in charge and buys back most of the deficit — ×2 signal-over-threshold takes the fleet to 93.1 % and the weakest pad from 64 % to 85 %. Noise scales with it and the ADC saturates earlier.
  • Lower sdt. Already at the campaign minimum on these chips; how much further it can go is set by the noise, which this measurement does not constrain. Needs a threshold scan (224 / 208 / 192) with beam.
  • Raise the mesh. Works — ×1.5 alone is worth 91.1 % — but it is multiplicative, so the gradient survives and the strong pads move too.
  • Use the per-channel trim DACs against the measured gain map. The right shape of fix, but the trim range is a few mV against a factor 3.9 in gain, and buying uniformity that way costs noise.
  • Fix the chamber. 0.42 relative rms, about 13 % per 10 mm, on the amplification side. This is the only option that helps both readouts.

Deliberately not on these three

Kept as backup slides so the main line stays one idea per slide:

  • The proof panel (figures/slide_proof.png) — the predicted-vs-measured scatter over all 53 pads, and the spread waterfall 0.42 → 0.36 → 0.26 that shows the threshold plus a +43-count additive ADC offset accounts for the compression with nothing left over.
  • The fix curve (figures/slide_fix.png) — efficiency against signal-over-threshold, all pads and the weakest pad, with 3.4× needed to reach DREAM's 95 %.
  • Caveats — the two runs are five days apart; the fitted level is a charge threshold and would absorb anything else that bites at the low end; the additive offset is measured, not explained; this is P2_OUT only; and nothing here measures the noise occupancy a lower threshold would bring. They are written out in the full note.
