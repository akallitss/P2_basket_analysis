What the P2 stations know about a track

P2 BASKET, SPS H4, July 2026. Three uRWELL pad stations at z = 320 / 630 / 940 mm, bracketed by two 1 mm-pitch EIC uRWELL reference planes 1370 mm apart. Run eff_nominal_1, 14 sub-runs, 2,226,918 reference tracks.

The three stations find the particle, and then lose it again. Detection is good — 0.894 / 0.909 / 0.955 of the reference tracks pointing at a live pad are recorded, and a three-station track exists for 78% of them. But every station reports a pad, not a position: the residual to the reference is a 12.1 mm box of rms 3.51 mm against the 3.48 mm a pad gives with no charge sharing at all, and a two-pad cluster does not improve it. The P2-only track therefore points to 4.3 mm at the exit of the basket and 0.76 mrad in the core, with a tail that reaches 8 mm at the 95th percentile.

And P2 cannot measure any of that on its own. Checking P2_MID against the line through P2_IN and P2_OUT returns 0.19 mm — 23× better than the 4.34 mm the reference measures on the same events. The reason is not subtle: 69% of the time all three stations report the identical pad, so the self-consistency residual is the same rounding error three times over.

What was compared

Nothing about the reference is new here. Tracks, the uRWELL → pad frame fit, the fiducial cut and the probe radius all come from urw_p2_efficiency.py with its defaults — two-point tracks with a 3 mm front↔back agreement cut, a fiducial of 9 mm to the nearest pad centre, and a 15 mm probe radius. The reference's own front↔back agreement is 0.77 mm core, so it contributes essentially nothing in quadrature to a 12 mm pad.

What is new is that all three stations are put in one frame at once. Each station's uRWELL → pad affine is inverted to bring its clusters back into the reference frame, and a straight line is fitted through the three. The alignment is therefore the reference's, which means every mean below is zero by construction and only the widths are a measurement. That is the honest configuration — an experiment would align against something external too — but nothing here bounds a global scale or rotation error. The fitted rotation is stable to 18 millidegrees across the 14 sub-runs, so nothing moved during the run.

Inside one pad

Folding every track onto the face of the pad it pointed at answers the obvious follow-up: is a hit lost at the pad edge, where the avalanche is split with the neighbour? No. Across the outer tenth of the face the leading pad keeps -5.8 % of its central charge and the cluster grows by +19 %, but the efficiency moves by +1.6 % — upwards, because at a boundary two pads each get a chance at the same charge. Everything inside 4 mm is flat to 1 %.

That is the same fact as the residual, seen from the other side. Sharing that only appears in the last millimetre, on 7% of clusters, cannot build a centroid — which is why the two-pad clusters in the third panel of the first figure are no better than the single-pad ones.

The one number to carry away. Stage 22's tag-and-probe, and any other check that uses P2 to judge P2, is looking at 69% identical pads. It cannot see the 4.34 mm the reference sees, and it cannot see the tail at all. On highstat_eff_1 the same effect shows up in the efficiency: 92.5 / 96.3 / 92.5 % self-referenced against 96.5 / 97.1 / 96.0 % against the uRWELL. The tracking version of that gap is 23×, not 4 points.

What this does not rule out

  • It does not bound a global alignment error. The uRWELL → pad affine is fitted per station on these same events, so a common rotation, scale or offset of the whole basket is absorbed, not measured. In the experiment that term is whatever the survey and the alignment procedure leave behind, and it adds to everything here.
  • It is one working point. All 14 sub-runs sit at the nominal mesh and drift of eff_nominal_1. Cluster size, and therefore the 7% of clusters that span two pads, grows with gain — a higher mesh would move the sharing question, though the 4.21 mm the two-pad clusters give today says it would have to move a long way to help.
  • The y pitch is an assumption. In x the measured rms lands on pitch/√12 to 0.9 %; in y it sits 8 % high. The pads are a fan described by radius and φ, so a single number for the y pitch is a simplification — the excess may be geometry rather than anything the detector is doing. It has not been chased.
  • It says nothing about timing. Only position and direction are measured here.
  • The tail is attributed, not proven. "One station picked the neighbouring pad" is what the shape looks like and what the same-pad fraction implies; it has not been separated from a genuine second particle in the trigger, though the second-cluster candidate (x2, y2) is already in the matcher to suppress exactly that.
  • Two-station tracks are not costed. 20% of tracks get exactly two stations, which is enough for a position and not for an angle; whether the experiment should use them is a separate question.
